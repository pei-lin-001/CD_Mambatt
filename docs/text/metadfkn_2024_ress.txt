A B S T R A C T
Reliable prediction of the remaining useful life (RUL) is important for improving maintenance efficiency,
equipment availability, and avoiding catastrophic accidents in complex industrial systems. Existing RUL
prediction models have made some contribution, relying mainly on a large amount of degraded data with
similar patterns or approximate distributions. However, in practical industrial systems, only a small amount of
labeling data is usually available, which may also come from different devices and different working conditions,
resulting in different distributions in the degraded data. This situation makes the existing RUL methods difficult
to achieve satisfactory generalization performance. To address this challenge, this paper proposes a novel
Meta-Learning with Deep Flow Kernel Network (MetaDFKN) model for RUL prediction under the few shot and
cross-domain conditions. The model first learns kernel features in a data-driven manner and considers them
as latent variables to improve the model’s representative ability of shared knowledge between tasks. Then,
we introduce the conditional normalized flow technique to infer richer posterior distributions in the kernel
features, which helps to obtain feature information with stronger discriminative power. Moreover, shared
knowledge and task-specific information are integrated into the contextual inference process, which can mine
the dependencies of related tasks and capture richer domain information. Finally, to evaluate the proposed
model, we conduct extensive experiments on engine and bearings degradation data, and the results verify the
superiority of the model.
1. Introduction
Prognostics and health management (PHM) plays an essential role
in modern industrial systems [1,2]. As the critical component of PHM,
remaining useful life (RUL) prediction aims to estimate the exact time
between the current health state of the machine and actual future fail-
ures [3]. To achieve accurate RUL prediction, numerous advanced solu-
tions have been devised. Currently, mainstream methods are generally
categorized into two categories: physics-modeled and data-driven [ 4].
Within the data-driven methods, deep learning (DL) methods serve as
a critical branch [ 5,6]. It exhibits profound representation ability and
can automatically learn critical features deeply hidden in the data,
which helps to enhance RUL prediction accuracy and generalization
performance [7,8].
At present, many DL methods have been employed for RUL predic-
tion for various devices [ 9]. Among them, recurrent neural networks
and their variant models are widely used. For example, the long short-
term memory (LSTM) network is combined with a multi-stage RUL pre-
diction model for aerospace engines [10]. The fuzzy c-means (FCM) and
the bidirectional LSTM are integrated for RUL online prediction [ 11].

Besides, being a hot topic in DL methods, the convolutional neural
network (CNN) is applied for RUL prediction of components [ 12]. For
example, a deep convolutional multi-scale feature fusion RUL predic-
tion model is proposed for aerospace engines [13]. Through a Bayesian
CNN, the solenoid valve current features are trained to predict the
remaining life of the solenoid valve (SV) [ 14]. Usually, existing DL
methods are mainly developed from statistical learning, while a strong
assumption of these methods is that degradation data should come from
the same distribution [15].
However, during the degradation process of the devices, the degra-
dation data collected from different devices or the same device under
different operating conditions are very likely to show different data
distributions with heterogeneity and imbalance due to the variation of
setting values, operating conditions, mechanical wear, etc., which can
be regarded as multi-domain data [ 16]. To address this issue, cross-
domain analysis is proposed as a new learning strategy [17] to achieve
RUL prediction by learning domain-invariant properties in a shared
latent space. Specifically, by training a prediction model on data with
specific working conditions (source domain) and then applying it to

At present, some methods for cross-domain prediction have been
developed, these cross-domain RUL prediction methods are grouped as
supervised learning and unsupervised learning [21,22]. In supervised
learning-based cross-domain analysis, the models usually need to be
trained by relying on source domain data and a limited number of
labeled data from the target domain, and then the model is applied
to the target domain for cross-domain prediction. Generally, the ma-
jority of RUL predictions primarily employ supervised cross-domain
analysis methods [23]. For example, RUL cross-domain prediction on
engine data is accomplished by using an adversarial domain adaptation
method, which can maximize the mutual information between the input
space and the latent space of the target domain data [24]. In [25],
a weighted domain adversarial neural network based on Wasserstein
distance for bearing RUL prediction is proposed, which uses domain
classifiers to measure sample quality, and learns representations from
samples with cross-domain similarity. These supervised learning-based
prediction methods have made some progress in cross-domain analysis,
but the limited amount of labeled data may not be sufficient to capture
the complexity and variability of the target domain. It increases the
difficulty of training the model, which leads to potential overfitting or
underfitting problems.
While unsupervised learning-based cross-domain analysis focuses
on training with completely unlabeled data in the target domain, it
can better adapt to variations among the data and learn feature rep-
resentations in the data, thus guiding the model to make cross-domain
predictions in the target domain [26]. Currently, the application of un-
supervised cross-domain analysis in RUL prediction is still a relatively
new research topic [27]. For example, A combined model consisting of
transformer and LSTM for engine RUL prediction is designed in [28],
which narrows the domain gap by aligning distributions at the feature
and semantic levels. In 2022, Miao 𝑒𝑡 𝑎𝑙. [29] designed a sparse domain
adaptation network for bearing RUL prediction, it combines unsuper-
vised sparse domain alignment and domain confrontation learning to
address the problem of data distribution shift. These methods have
made some improvements in unsupervised cross-domain prediction,
but they require unlabeled samples from the target domain for model
training, which still has some limitations in practical applications. This
disparity arises from the inherent heterogeneity of the data, which can
vary in format, structure, and granularity across different domains.
Consequently, the application of unsupervised learning methods be-
comes intricate due to the unique preprocessing and feature extraction
requirements imposed by individual datasets.
In addition, in existing cross-domain RUL prediction methods, large-
scale labeled data are usually required for model training. However, in
most practical applications, data collection and annotation are time-
consuming and expensive [30]. In other words, only a tiny fraction of
a large amount of data is labeled and available at any time, that is few-
shot learning problem [31]. The most common solution is to use data
augmentation techniques to expand the dataset by making various mod-
ifications to the data, such as adding Gaussian noise, masking noise,
and signal translation [32,33]. To some extent, these methods can
improve the model’s recognition accuracy, but if the augmented data
is too similar to the original data, which will result in overfitting. This
could be a particular issue in cross-domain analysis, where augmented
data might not accurately represent the target domain. Surprisingly,
meta-learning [34] is a highly adaptable strategy that focuses on how
to acquire the ability to learn, rather than the itself of learning task.
It is good at leveraging the knowledge learned from previous tasks
to improve performance on new and unseen tasks with few training
examples [35,36].
Meta-learning has been widely studied in industrial system fault
diagnosis, but it is still in the exploratory stage for remaining useful
life prediction. Mo 𝑒𝑡 𝑎𝑙. [37] employs a model agnostic meta-learning
(MAML) algorithm to find suitable initialization of the model pa-
rameters, and then calculates the time series similarity to construct
a pseudo-meta-rule task set of meta-learning for RUL prediction of
the engine. Pan 𝑒𝑡 𝑎𝑙 . [38] combines meta-learning and multi-head
attentional networks for rule prediction, combining convolution-based
and attentional branching for rocket engine RUL prediction. Further, an
iterative meta-network pruning algorithm is proposed to automatically
compute the meta-gradient of each convolutional kernel based on the
chain rule to realize the RUL prediction for rocket motors [39]. These
meta-learning-based models improve the RUL prediction accuracy to
some extent, but may exhibit limited generalization ability when ap-
plied to datasets with significantly different features or under different
operating conditions [40]. Besides, in extreme cases, data from the
target domain may be unlabeled, while models trained only on the
source domain may be biased towards the source domain, increasing
prediction errors. Meanwhile, there may also be considerable variation
in the data distribution between the source and target domains, making
the model difficult to adapt to the new domain, resulting in poor
prediction performance.
In order to address the aforementioned problems, this paper pro-
poses a novel MetaDFKN model that is based on a Meta-learning with
Deep Flow Kernel Network for few shot cross-domain RUL prediction.
The key process is to combine the meta-Gaussian with the MAML frame-
work to obtain the kernel feature through the data-driven method, on
which it performs flow processing to capture more expressive feature
information. Then, a tiny contextual inference network is designed
by using fewer parameters to memorize shared knowledge and prior
information from previous tasks, making it more sensitive to contex-
tual information. Thereby, an effective RUL prediction in a few shot
cross-domain scenario is realized.
The main contributions are summarized as follows:
• For the few shot cross-domain scenario, a MetaDFKN model based
on the meta-learning framework is proposed for RUL prediction.
In particular, we design a tiny contextual memory network to
acquire rich domain information, which learns meta-knowledge
through fast and accurate contextual inference on multiple related
tasks. Because of this design, our model can use fewer parameters
and quickly adapt to new tasks.
• The technique of conditional normalized flows is integrated into
the Gaussian process regression model of cross-domain RUL pre-
diction with fewer training samples, which can effectively en-
hance the expressiveness of variational posteriors. Thus, the
model is guaranteed to capture critical information even in the
few shot cross-domain scenario and produce accurate prediction
results.
• Through the verification of extensive experiments the proposed
MetaDFKN model shows superior performance than other ad-
vanced methods in cross-domain RUL prediction, demonstrating
its great potential for solving complex few shot cross-domain RUL
problems.
The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. Section 2 de-
tails the implementation of the MetaDFKN model. Section 3 introduces
the two datasets and their preprocessing. Section 4 performs typical
experiments and analysis to evaluate the MetaDFKN model and other
advanced methods. The conclusions and future works are presented in
Section 5.

3.1. Overview of the datasets
3.1.1. C-MAPSS dataset
NASA widely uses the C-MAPSS dataset as a baseline for PHM [24].
As shown in Fig. 2 , it is a multi-dimensional data set collected from
multiple sensors to monitor the degradation process of an aircraft
engine in real time. The data come from the low pressure compressor
(LPC), the low pressure turbine (LPT), the high pressure compressor
(HPC), the high pressure turbine (HPT), the fan and the combustion
chamber in the engine. A total of 21 sensor devices are set up to monitor
the speed, the temperature and the pressure, which are deployed at
different collection points to track the degradation state of engines in
real time.
The C-MAPSS dataset includes four datasets, Table 1 shows the
detailed parameters of four datasets. FD001 and FD003 both have 100
training units and 100 test units, FD002 has 260 training units and 259
test units, and FD004 has 248 training units and 249 test units [24]. For
every training unit, it is customary to produce a bit of initial wearing
at the beginning. Then it starts to degrade gradually as the runtime
increases, until it fails. For every test unit, the recording sequence will
stop at some time before the system failure, so maintaining the actual
RUL of the test unit. The training set composed of training units is used
to train the prediction model, and the test set composed of test units is
used for performance evaluation.
3.1.2. PRONOSTIA dataset
The PRONOSTIA dataset is sampled from the bearing test platform,
which is applied for accelerated degradation experiments. As shown in
Fig. 3, the platform contains load part, rotating part and data collection
part. Among them, the PRONOSTIA test platform has a sample time of
0.1s, a sampling frequency of 25.6 kHz, a motor power of 250w, and
2560 data points are captured every 10 seconds [ 25]. The dataset is
collected without adding human failures, and all of them are the natural
degradation process of bearings, which can better match with the actual
industrial scenarios. The entire accelerated degradation experiment
includes almost all failure types such as inner race, roller, cage and
outer race, and the detailed description of each experiment is shown in
Table 2.

This section evaluates the RUL prediction performance of our
MetaDFKN model in terms of RMSE and SCORE metrics by comparing

4.1. Experimental parameters analysis
4.1.1. Flow kernel lengths
For the MetaDFKN model, the flow lengths 𝐾 are set as 2, 4, 6, 8,
10, 12, 14, 16, 18, and 20 to analyze the prediction accuracy. Since
the experimental results of the PRONOSTIA dataset are roughly similar
to those of the C-MAPSS dataset, i.e., the 𝐾 values are selected consis-
tently. Hence, the experimental results of 12 cross-domain scenarios in
the C-MAPSS dataset are selected for presentation, as shown in Fig. 4.
It can be observed from Fig. 4 there are fluctuations in RMSE
and SCORE as 𝐾 changes, rather than a strict monotonic increase
followed by a monotonic decrease. This is caused by the random
initialization parameters of the meta-learning model and differences
in data distribution. Besides, it is found that the MetaDFKN model
shows excellent performance at 𝐾 = 10 in most scenarios. As the value
of 𝐾 increases, the computational resources and time of the model
gradually increase due to the increase in the layers of flow kernel
and the model parameters, but the prediction effect of the model does
not improve significantly. Therefore, we finally choose 𝐾 = 10 as the
optimal parameter for flow kernel length.
4.1.2. Number of network layers
The hidden layers of TinyCMN 𝐿 are set as 1, 3, 5, 7, and 9 to ana-
lyze our model performance. Similarly, since the experimental results of
the PRONOSTIA dataset are roughly similar to those of the C-MAPSS
dataset, the experimental results of the 12 cross-domain scenarios in
the C-MAPSS dataset are selected for presentation. Fig. 5 shows the
experimental results of the RMSE and SCORE with the increase of
the hidden layer (from 1 to 9). It can be observed that the property
of TinyCMN is in the decrease state as the increase of hidden layer
𝐿, but there are some differences under different scenarios. For the
scenarios of FD001 →FD004 and FD004 →FD001, the curves change
sharply because the FD001 dataset has a single fault type and the
FD004 dataset has two fault types. Additionally, the two datasets have
significant differences, and the limited training data is easy to cause
overfitting, leading to the prediction performance decreases. Hence, we
finally use the single GRU layer considering the property of our model.

TMSATCN [48]. According to the experimental setup scheme of the
above latest methods, these models are first trained with the dataset
of the source domain, and then they are fine-tuned with a limited
quantity of data from the target domain, as ‘‘Target-Unsupervised’’. The
experimental parameters and results of existing methods come from
Refs. [22–25,28,29,45,47,48].
Using RMSE and SCORE as evaluation metrics, the performance of
different methods is evaluated in different cross-domain scenarios.
Tables 6 and 7 present the test results on the C-MAPSS dataset.
Tables 8 and 9 present the test results on the PRONOSTIA dataset. In
general, the proposed MetaDFKN model is more outstanding than other
advanced methods. Furthermore, we analyzed the best method among
the reported methods and compared its results with MetaDFKN. On the
C-MAPSS dataset, the average improvement rate results demonstrate
that the MetaDFKN model outperforms the DATransformer method
by over 36% and 55% on RMSE and SCORE, respectively. On the
PRONOSTIA dataset, we compare the results of TMSATCN method and
the RMSE and SCORE of MetaDFKN model exceed 86% and 58%,
respectively. Through the above comparisons, the MetaDFKN model
can transfer knowledge between complex data sets of different degrees
and show excellent performance in cross-domain RUL prediction.
5. Conclusions
This paper presents a novel MetaDFKN model based on meta-
learning to address the few shot cross-domain problem in RUL pre-
diction. The model can effectively realize few shot cross-domain pre-
diction, and has strong knowledge transfer adaptability. The model
gains the shared knowledge in the latent space in the form of implicit
modeling, and obtains a more expressive flow kernel through flow
processing. Second, we design a tiny contextual inference method
that integrates prior shared knowledge and task-specific information
into the current task. Then, the previous shared knowledge and prior
information are memorized by the tiny inference network. Finally, with
the help of probabilistic modeling, a new RUL prediction about the few
shot cross-domain task is learned by using the variational posterior and
meta-knowledge. The effectiveness of the model is validated with the
publicly available C-MAPSS and PRONOSTIA datasets. Experimental
results show that the proposed MetaDFKN model has superior perfor-
mance in few shot cross-domain RUL prediction. In the future, we will
